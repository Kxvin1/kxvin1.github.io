Beat 1 · The problem

Thousands of failing E2E tests a day, and new ones written slowly, all by hand.

I lead a team that investigates 5,000+ E2E test failures a day for about 200 customers.5 Every failure meant someone reading its logs, its trace and the live page to find the cause, then deciding: is the E2E test wrong, or is the product broken? New E2E tests were slow too: the company expected about 10 a week.3 And every E2E test, new or a teammate's, needed a person to check that it does what its steps say.

Beat 2 · What I built

Mission Control: agents that investigate, create and review E2E tests, and a person on every call that is hard to undo.

A personal project, 2026 to present.6 Three kinds of agent work share one evidence gate and one person on the calls. A lead agent session owns each client, and worker sessions each own one piece of work end to end.4

  1. The workInvestigate Failures grouped by cause on the Investigations page, each root cause with evidenceCreate New E2E tests, every written step mapped to an assertion seen passingReview Each E2E test checked against its steps; a second agent challenges every verdict
  2. AgentsEach kind of work has its own agents, and every claim carries its evidence
  3. Lead per clientWorkers each own one piece, end to end
  4. Evidence gate"Ready" needs a clean local run plus a platform run, same code
  5. A personApproves only what is hard to undo

↻Every correction I make becomes a written rule, and where it can, a check that fails on a repeat.
